# Production architecture and acceptance contract

Status: required target, not implemented or certified. ROADMAP.md remains the overall acceptance plan. This contract applies equally to historical and live data. Preserve all existing evidence and the public beta while migrating incrementally; no rewrite solely for architectural appearance.

## Architecture and migration

1. Discovery: a primary transfer-index feed is a hint, not the completeness boundary. Discover master and wallet chains independently and connect message endpoints, including wallets with zero balances, mint/burn, bounced/failed/refunded operations and new wallets. For live processing use resumable LT/hash and finalized masterchain/shard boundaries; store the scope and exact provenance of each cursor. Do not present an independently unverified timestamp as a common block. The fixed historical anchor is a bootstrap checkpoint, not a permanent end of verification.
2. Evidence: shared append-only raw transaction/message/state observations, including body/BOC where needed, provider identity, retrieval time, block references and digests. Deduplicate by canonical chain identifiers, not by amounts or owner alone. Distinguish absent, contradictory and unsupported evidence. Preserve legacy immutable records and their lineage. Missing observations must enter durable retry/review queues; a blocked observation is not a resolved fact. Record new evidence as a new version rather than overwrite an old failure.
3. Verification: one reusable implementation for historical replay and live ingestion, validating chain continuity, finality, message causality, wallet/master/owner identity at the relevant state, and successful delivery. Independent discovery must not depend exclusively on traces found by the primary provider. Agreement of two indexed views is not a cryptographic block proof. Define which proof levels support which metrics.
4. Protocol engine: canonical raw message graph input; adapters keyed by proven contract/protocol/version/opcode and causal settlement, not hardcoded individual transaction hashes. Existing repair modules and overlays are migration inputs, not the permanent parallel classification system. Cover STON.fi v1/v2, DeDust legacy/v2, moon.cx CPMM/CLMM, Omniston, any observed routed protocol and mint/burn/transfer, LP join/exit, fees, refunds, failed/bounced, arbitrage, split and multihop. Unknown operations are retained as unknown, not forced into buys/sells. Each rule has version, evidence links, positive/negative real fixtures and explicit ambiguity handling. Reprocess affected historical AND live events when rules change.
5. Canonical ledger: deterministic event identity, exact integer nano-units, ordered account transitions, no double counting of intermediate legs. Separate observed movements, verified accounting and economic labels. Reconcile token supply, per-wallet balances and in-flight messages at a common boundary, account for mint/burn and documented exceptions. Analytics, PnL and ranking use only sufficient evidence; display incompleteness and suppress unsupported metrics, not invent zeros.
6. Derived product: single ledger-derived analytics, reproducible releases pinned to source/evidence/rule versions, UI shows discovery/verification/classification watermarks separately, last successful update and scope. Preserve last validated release on failed validation. No AI or manual per-transaction intervention in normal runtime.

## Priority and implementation gates

A. Let current bounded backfill finish its checkpoint, then audit existing ingestion, evidence, adapters, ledger, scheduler and deployment dependencies. Produce an inventory of what is shared, duplicated, diagnostic-only or missing, with actual tests and measured capacity. Do not continue indefinite isolated repair loops without mapping their role in the shared pipeline.
B. Specify canonical contracts, identifiers, proof levels, cursors, queues, adapter interfaces, migration and rollback. Implement a vertical slice from raw observation through ledger to a derived metric using the same code for historical replay and live ingestion. Verify historical/live parity on identical inputs and cutoff. Preserve legacy data; compare shadow outputs before cutover.
C. Generalize the historical discoveries and classifiers, bridge the fixed anchor to a continuously moving finalized boundary, independently discover newly activated wallets, and converge backlog while maintaining fresh ingestion. Demonstrate replay from deployment and catch-up after interruptions, without gaps/duplicate ledger events.
D. Migrate analytics only after reconciliation gates, then verify autonomous operation. Architecture gate does not discard already collected proofs; historical backfill can continue safely in parallel.

## Production acceptance (mandatory, not current claims)

- Replay determinism: identical pinned raw inputs + rule version produce identical canonical results and digest, independent of arrival order, retries, or batch boundaries. A historical/live parity regression and replay from a clean checkout are required. Rule upgrade invalidates stale derived results and recomputes affected data.
- Coverage: compare independent discovery, chain closure, source reconciliation, mint/burn, unresolved queues and historical supply/balance at a common boundary. A known unsupported fact cannot be marked verified. Report each denominator and proof scope independently.
- Resilience: inject provider outage, 429, timeout, incomplete pages, duplicate and late arrivals (including beyond current overlap), revised provider rows, unknown protocol/code, malformed evidence, interrupted write, concurrent jobs and stale deployment. Assert no silent loss, no fabricated labels, no double-count and safe recovery; record last good checkpoint, error, retry/backoff and backlog age. Test actual restore from external backup.
- Freshness and throughput: measure real scheduled executions, not just configured cron or pushes. Target verified publication lag <=45 min under ordinary conditions; explicitly distinguish data lag, audit lag and UI lag. Track arrival/processing rate, queue depth/age, API calls/limits, run duration, storage growth and failed stages; show stale status when missed. Require at least 72 continuous hours of unattended observation (extends the original 24h minimum), including a controlled interruption/catch-up and real new-data processing. A green workflow alone is not acceptance.
- Infrastructure: audit current shared writer concurrency (queued runs may be replaced), GitHub cron delays, raw data growth, clone/replay cost, storage and Actions quotas. Separate acquisition and publication where practical without unsafe concurrent writes. Keep GitHub Pages if suitable; compare free/low-cost alternatives for ingestion, scheduling and storage using measured reliability, cost, and recoverability. No mandatory paid infrastructure or migration without evidence and owner approval of recurring cost.
- Final release: no unexplained critical coverage/ledger defects; independently checked wallet examples and all discovered operation classes; automated monitoring, explicit uncertainty, mobile/desktop browser tests, source/data reproducibility, documented recovery and owner approval. Any unproven criterion keeps the product beta. No claim of future infallibility; new protocol versions enter the same unknown/review pipeline.

## Current verified gaps (2026-11-28)

Current incremental.py reads Toncenter transfer windows with one-hour overlap, appends normalized batches and classifies provider actions. It does not independently enumerate every new wallet/master chain or capture every raw payload. current_audit.py compares endpoint swap multisets on primary-discovered trace revisions, not independent discovery. genesis backfill pins a fixed timestamp and is not yet a live cursor. Specialized repair, LP and arbitrage modules are not a unified canonical ledger. Actual schedule records show main recovery runs at 23:20 UTC Nov 27 and 04:46 UTC Nov 28 despite a 15-minute configured cron. This is an operational reliability gap, not proof of an exact root cause. Existing tests and checkpoints are valuable but do not certify production readiness.
